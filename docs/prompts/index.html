Guide 8

The prompt library

Prompts you can copy, edit the brackets, and send. Each one follows a recipe Microsoft publishes.

The short version

  • A prompt has four parts: goal (required), context, source, expectations.
  • Replace everything in [square brackets].
  • If the first result is off, say what to change instead of starting again.
  • Pick the section for your track: Chat or Premium.
About these prompts

I wrote them from Microsoft's four-part pattern. I have not run each one against every Copilot version, and results differ by licence and by what is in your files. Treat them as starting points, then keep the ones that work for you.

The recipe

PartWhat to sayRequired?
GoalThe task you want doneYes
ContextWhy you need it and who it is forNo
SourceWhat to draw on: your own pasted text, a file, or a web pageNo
ExpectationsFormat, tone, lengthNo

Source: Microsoft (2026c).

Habits worth keeping

  • Instruct it not to guess. "If it's not in the document, say so" works better than hoping.
  • Fix, don't restart. "Shorter, and less formal" is a complete follow-up.
  • Don't paste what you shouldn't. Read Safety first.

ChatPrompts for Copilot Chat

Paste the text, or upload or select the file first. Chat does not search your files for you.

Reply to a difficult email

Prompt
Draft a reply to the email below.
Context: the sender is [role], and I want to [agree / decline / buy time] without sounding defensive.
Keep it under 120 words, polite and direct, and end with one clear next step.

[Paste the email here]

Make something shorter without losing the point

Prompt
Rewrite the text below for [audience] in about half the length.
Keep every number and date. Use plain words and short sentences.
After the rewrite, list anything you removed that they might still ask about.

[Paste the text here]

Summarise an attached document for a decision

Prompt
Using only the attached document, summarise it for a busy manager who must decide [what].
Give me: the three most important points, any risks or open questions, and what is missing from the document.
If something is not in the document, say so rather than guessing.

Turn notes into actions

Prompt
From these notes, list the action items as a table with columns: action, owner, due date.
If an owner or date is not stated, write "not stated" rather than inventing one.

[Paste the notes here]

Prepare for a meeting

Prompt
I have a [length]-minute meeting with [who] about [topic]. My goal is [outcome].
Suggest an agenda with timings, three questions I should ask, and two objections I should be ready for.
Keep it to one page.

Challenge my plan

Prompt
Here is my plan: [paste or describe it].
Act as a sceptical colleague. Tell me the three weakest assumptions, what could go wrong in the first month, and one thing I haven't considered.
Be specific, not generic.

When you don't know what to ask

Prompt
I want help with [task] but I'm not sure how to ask.
Ask me up to five questions, one at a time, to understand what I need.
When you have enough, write the best prompt for the job and then answer it.

PremiumPrompts for M365 Copilot (Premium)

Name the file, person or project instead of pasting. Copilot finds it, within what you can already open.

Reply to a difficult email

Prompt
Draft a reply to the open email from [name].
Context: I want to [agree / decline / buy time] without sounding defensive. Check our earlier emails on this so the reply is consistent.
Keep it under 120 words, polite and direct, and end with one clear next step.

Summarise a file for a decision

Prompt
Using only /[file name], summarise it for a busy manager who must decide [what].
Give me: the three most important points, any risks or open questions, and what is missing from the document.
If something is not in the document, say so rather than guessing.

Catch me up on a project

Prompt
Catch me up on [project] from the last [two weeks]: key decisions, what is blocked, and what is waiting on me.
Use my emails, meetings and files. Name the source for each point.

Prepare for a meeting

Prompt
I have a [length]-minute meeting with [who] about [topic]. My goal is [outcome].
Use our recent emails and shared files to suggest an agenda with timings, three questions I should ask, and two objections I should be ready for.
Keep it to one page.

Turn meeting notes into actions

Prompt
From the notes in /[file name], list the action items as a table with columns: action, owner, due date.
If an owner or date is not stated, write "not stated" rather than inventing one.

Challenge my plan

Prompt
Here is my plan: /[file name].
