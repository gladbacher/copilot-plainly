Technique

The 12 moves

Most weak answers come from weak asks. These twelve habits work on every licence, and each one takes a sentence.

The short version

  • Say the goal, who it is for, what to use and what you want back. Microsoft's own recipe has those four parts.
  • Treat the first answer as a draft and reply to it.
  • Make Copilot question you, criticise itself and admit what it doesn't know.

Asking well

1. Lead with the goal

Start with the task and its purpose. The goal is the only part Microsoft calls required.

Move 1
Write a short update for [audience] so they can decide whether to [decision].

2. Say who it is for

Audience changes vocabulary, length and what gets explained.

Move 2
The reader is [who]. They know [what they already know] and care most about [what they care about].

3. Hand it the source

Point at the material and fence it in. "Using only" stops it filling gaps from elsewhere.

Move 3
Using only [the text below / the attached file], and nothing else, answer this: [question]

4. Describe the output

Format, length and tone. If you want a table, say which columns.

Move 4
Give me a table with columns [column names]. No more than [8] rows. Plain words, no jargon.

5. Show an example

One sample of what good looks like beats a paragraph describing it.

Move 5
Here is an example of the style I want:
[Paste an example here]
Write the new one in the same style and length.

Steering the answer

6. Reply, don't restart

Microsoft expects you to refine. A follow-up keeps everything it already knows.

Move 6
Shorter, less formal, and put the recommendation first.

7. Ask for options

Three versions let you choose instead of accept.

Move 7
Give me three versions: one cautious, one direct, one persuasive. Say which you would send and why.

8. Make it interview you

When you can't describe what you need, answer questions instead.

Move 8
I want help with [task]. Ask me up to five questions, one at a time. When you have enough, write the best prompt for the job and then answer it.

9. Work in steps

Outline, then draft, then polish. You catch a wrong turn early.

Move 9
Step 1 only: give me an outline. Wait for my OK before you write anything else.

Trusting the answer

10. Forbid guessing

Responses are not guaranteed to be factual. Give it permission to say it doesn't know.

Move 10
If the answer is not in the source, say "not stated". Do not guess or fill gaps.

11. Make it criticise its own work

It is better at finding faults when asked than at avoiding them unasked.

Move 11
Now act as a sceptical reviewer. List the three weakest points in what you just wrote, then fix them.

12. Ask where it came from

Then open the source. For anything that matters, run the prompt twice: answers vary.

Move 12
For each claim, show where it comes from: quote the line or give the link. Mark anything you could not verify.

Keep what works

When a prompt works, save it. Hover over it in Copilot and look for the bookmark icon, Save prompt. Or keep a note or a document called "My prompts" and paste the good ones in, with the blanks marked in [square brackets]. In a month you will have your own library that fits your job better than any website's.

Tired of repeating your preferences? Put them in custom instructions once.
