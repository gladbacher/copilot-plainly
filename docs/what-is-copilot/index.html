Guide 1

What Copilot is, and what it costs

"Copilot" is the name on many different Microsoft products. This page sorts out the ones you meet at work.

The short version

  • This site covers two tiers: Chat (free with eligible plans) and Premium (the paid add-on).
  • The main difference is whether Copilot can reach your work data on its own.
  • Microsoft recently renamed things. Old and new names are both in use.
  • Your employer buys and assigns the paid licence, not you.

The names changed

Microsoft's own documentation now says that Microsoft 365 Copilot is called Microsoft Copilot, and Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat is called Microsoft Copilot Chat. Pages and menus still use the old names during the transition (Microsoft, 2026a). The web address is now copilot.cloud.microsoft.

In practice: if one article says "Microsoft 365 Copilot" and another says "Microsoft Copilot" they are usually describing the same product. Do not mix it up with the free consumer Copilot or with GitHub Copilot, which is for programmers. Those are separate.

Under your name you will see one of three labels (Microsoft, 2026b). On this site:

  • Copilot Chat (Basic) and M365 Copilot (Basic) are the Chat track.
  • M365 Copilot (Premium) is the Premium track.

Usage-based extras such as Copilot Cowork and Copilot Code need the Premium licence and are billed by consumption. They are not covered yet; a later section will cover them.

ChatWith Copilot Chat

What you get: a secure AI chat included with eligible Microsoft 365 plans when you sign in with a work or school account (Microsoft, 2026e). It works from the web, and also in Outlook and Teams.

Two Chat labels, and Word/Excel/PowerPoint. Microsoft's overview says people with the Copilot Chat (Basic) label "don't have access" to Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote, while people with M365 Copilot (Basic) have standard access to it, subject to service capacity (Microsoft, 2026a, 2026s). Microsoft's Learn requirements page adds that chat in Word, Excel and PowerPoint varies with licence and tenant configuration (Microsoft, 2026aq).

What it can see: Chat cannot use Microsoft Graph, the layer that holds your emails, files, meetings and calendar. It can use your content only if you hand it over: paste it, upload a file, or open it next to Copilot in Outlook or Teams. Copilot only shows you data you already have at least view permission for (Microsoft, 2026d).

What I could not confirm

Several university help pages, and a secondary quote of a Microsoft Message Center notice, say the Copilot pane in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote ended for unlicensed users from 15 April 2026. Microsoft's current pages are consistent with that for the Copilot Chat (Basic) label, but I couldn't find the original announcement on a Microsoft documentation page. I also couldn't find a page that explains what decides whether you get the Copilot Chat (Basic) or the M365 Copilot (Basic) label. The reliable test is your own apps: open Word and look for the Copilot button. If it is missing, work down this checklist.

PremiumWith M365 Copilot (Premium)

What you get: the paid add-on. Everything in Chat, plus priority access, Copilot Search, agents that use work data, and management tools for administrators (Microsoft, 2026a).

What it can see: it pulls from Graph and Work IQ automatically. You can name a file to focus on, but you do not have to upload it. An administrator can switch Work IQ off, in which case answers are not enriched with your work context (Microsoft, 2026a). It still only shows you data you already have at least view permission to open (Microsoft, 2026d). To check whether Work IQ is working for you, ask Copilot about a file you know you can open; if it can't find it, see Troubleshooting.

The cost reality check

You probably do not pay for any of this directly.

Copilot Chat is included at no extra cost with eligible Microsoft 365 plans when you sign in with a work or school account (Microsoft, 2026e). The paid licence is an add-on that your employer buys and an administrator assigns.

If you work for a small business, or you are the person deciding, these are Microsoft's listed prices for the Copilot Business add-on. They are US dollars per user per month, excluding tax, checked again on 6 November 2026 with the same figures (Microsoft, 2026f). I did not check UK prices, which differ:

  • The $18 rate runs from 1 July to 31 December 2026, applies to the first year only, and is for existing customers on an eligible Business plan (not trials).
  • Annual commitment, with a 7-day window for a prorated refund.

Prices for larger enterprise plans are on Microsoft's Copilot page. I have not listed them here because I could not confirm them on a Microsoft page.

Check your own setup

  1. Which label do I see under my name at copilot.cloud.microsoft?
  2. Is my licence assigned? Microsoft publishes a licence details page at aka.ms/CopilotLicenseDetails.
  3. Do I see the Work label and green shield?
  4. Is there a View info from your organization link in the app? That is where your organisation puts its own guidance (Microsoft, 2026e).

Web search, agents and models are organisation settings. You cannot change them, but you can see what is switched on by what appears in your own menus.
