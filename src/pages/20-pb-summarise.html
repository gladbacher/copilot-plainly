Playbook

Summarise a long document

Ask for a summary built for a decision, and make Copilot say what the document leaves out. What a document omits is often louder than what it says.

ChatWith Copilot Chat

  1. Give it the documentUpload the file or select it when you write the prompt (Microsoft, 2026a). Chat does not go and find it for you.
  2. Ask for a purpose-built summarySay who is reading and what they must decide.
  3. Reading a web page or PDF?Open it in Edge and select the Copilot icon. Copilot Chat can summarise what is on screen (Microsoft, 2026a).
Prompt · Chat
Using only the attached document, summarise it for [who], who must decide [what].
Give me: the three most important points, the risks or open questions, and what is missing from the document.
If something is not in the document, say so. Do not guess.

PremiumWith M365 Copilot (Premium)

  1. Name the fileNo upload needed. Copilot can open anything you can (Microsoft, 2026a, 2026d).
  2. Ask for a purpose-built summarySay who is reading and what they must decide.
  3. Compare documentsName two files and ask what changed between them.

Using only /[file name], summarise it for [who], who must decide [what].
Give me: the three most important points, the risks or open questions, and what is missing from the document.

Push it further

The first answer is a draft. Send one of these next:

Follow-up
List every date, number and commitment in a table, with the page or section.
Follow-up
What are the three weakest arguments in this document?
Follow-up
Write five questions I should ask the author.

Before you use it

  • Open the document at two or three of the points and confirm they are there.
  • Summaries flatten caveats. Read the section behind anything you will act on.
  • Ask "where does that come from?" for any surprising claim.

Results vary by licence and by your files. If a prompt misses, reply to Copilot with what to change.
