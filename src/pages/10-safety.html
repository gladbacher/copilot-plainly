Guide 9

Safety and privacy

The honest version of "is it safe?". What is protected, what is not, and what is up to you.

The short version

  • Copilot shows you only what you could already open. It does not break permissions.
  • Microsoft says your prompts, responses and work data are not used to train its foundation models.
  • Your chats are stored, and your employer's admins can find them.
  • The real risk is files that are shared too widely in your company, and answers that are wrong.

1What Copilot can see

Copilot reaches your work data through Microsoft Graph and surfaces only items you have at least view permission for. That includes people outside your company who have been given access through collaboration features such as shared channels in Teams (Microsoft, 2026d).

It also honours sensitivity labels and encryption that your company applies. If a file's usage rights stop you from using it, they stop Copilot too (Microsoft, 2026d).

2The real risk: oversharing

Copilot does not give you extra access. But it makes content you already can reach much easier to find. A file in a SharePoint site that "everyone" can open was always technically visible to you; Copilot can now surface it from a plain question.

Microsoft's own advice for companies is to tighten permissions, and it offers tools such as SharePoint Advanced Management for reducing oversharing (Microsoft, 2026a). Two things you can do:

  • Share files with specific people, not "anyone with the link" or whole-company groups.
  • If Copilot shows you something that you think you should not see, tell IT instead of using it.

3What is stored, and who can see it

  • Your prompts, Copilot's responses and the citations are stored as your Copilot activity history. It is encrypted and handled under the same commitments as your company's other Microsoft 365 content (Microsoft, 2026d).
  • Administrators can search and set retention for that history using tools such as Content search and Microsoft Purview. Assume a prompt is as visible to your employer as an email.
  • You can delete your own activity history in the My Account portal (myaccount.microsoft.com).

4What Microsoft says it does not do

  • Prompts, responses and data accessed through Microsoft Graph are not used to train the foundation models (Microsoft, 2026d).
  • Copilot services have opted out of human abuse-monitoring review (Microsoft, 2026d).
  • Copilot is restricted from making judgments about an employee's performance, attitude, emotional state or personal characteristics from their workplace communication (Microsoft, 2026d).

These are Microsoft's statements about its commercial service when you sign in with a work account. Personal-account Copilot has separate terms.

5What not to paste

Check your company policy first

Technical protections do not replace your employer's rules. Ask if there are any, then apply them.

  • Anything your company classifies as restricted, or that a legal, HR or security rule says must not leave a system.
  • Other people's personal details you have no reason to process, such as health, performance or disciplinary information.
  • Passwords, keys and access tokens.
  • Anything into the personal version of Copilot. If you do not see Work and the green shield, you are not in the protected experience.

6Wrong answers

Microsoft says outputs are not guaranteed to be fully factual and that you should use your judgement before sharing them (Microsoft, 2026d). Treat Copilot like a fast colleague who sometimes confidently gets it wrong:

  • Check names, numbers, dates and quotes against the source.
  • Ask it to cite where each claim came from, then open the citation.
  • Be most careful with meeting summaries and anything that goes to a client.

7Web search and agents

  • When Copilot searches the web it turns parts of your prompt into a query sent to Bing. Don't include things in a prompt that you would not type into a search engine (Microsoft, 2026d, 2026e).
  • Agents built by other companies have their own privacy statements. Your admin decides which are allowed. If you are given one, read how it handles your data (Microsoft, 2026d).

8Questions to ask IT

  1. Is there a company policy on what can be shared with Copilot?
  2. How long do you keep Copilot chat history?
  3. Is web search switched on, and which agents are allowed?
  4. Have our SharePoint permissions been reviewed for oversharing?
